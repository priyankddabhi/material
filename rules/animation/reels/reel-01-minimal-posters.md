## Visual — what it looks like

- Fast-cut montage of minimalist poster-style designs, vertical 9:16 frame.
- Restricted palette: black, white, light-to-mid grey gradients, and ONE vivid red accent.
- Backgrounds mostly flat or softly vignetted grey, or solid black, with generous negative space.
- Recurring motifs: one large flat red circle behind the subject, thin black rules/frame lines, tiny side/corner print, QR code, barcode, dotted red arcs, small red dots/crosses.
- Subjects are cut-out photographs, not live footage: a silhouetted man, a man at a desk, two men in a film still, an astronaut in profile, an animated fox/wolf character.
- Minimalism = one subject, one accent shape, very little text per frame.

## Motion — how it moves

- Hard cuts between posters — no dissolve clearly seen — but motion inside each poster makes cuts feel continuous.
- Elements slide in from side or bottom, fade in, and scale up with a soft pop/overshoot: red circle scales in behind the subject, main word scales up from small to large, secondary words fade/slide in after it.
- Large background words drift/slide horizontally across the frame, creating parallax against the static foreground subject.
- One character slides in from the right edge.
- No live camera movement. Simulated camera instead: slow push-in/zoom on posters, horizontal drift of oversized background type.

## Font and size

- Clean modern sans-serif throughout, mostly lower-case.
- Hierarchy almost entirely by size and weight, NOT by changing typeface: tiny thin caption-size labels in corners/edges, medium light lower-case for lead-in words, very large bold lower-case for the punch word.
- Words often start small/grey and grow darker/bolder as they are emphasised.
- Oversized background lettering is cropped by the frame edge, so only fragments are readable at any moment — that is intentional, it creates depth, not a mistake.

## Timing / pacing (estimates)

- Poster changes every roughly 2–4 seconds.
- Inside each poster, text builds in stages: small lead-in, then large punch word, roughly 1 second apart, so the eye is never left still.
- Complete pass through the designs seen took roughly 20–30 seconds, but that is an estimate, not a verified duration.

## Why it feels premium

- Consistency: same red circle, same grey/black/white palette, same lower-case sans-serif, same layout logic (subject + circle + one phrase + tiny print details) across very different images.
- Generous empty space around every element.
- Soft easing, not jerky jumps: elements arrive, settle, then the next phrase grows, rather than flashing.
- Single red accent draws the eye to the subject.
- Poster-authentic details (frame rules, QR/barcode, stereo label, small side text) make each frame look like a finished print design, not a random collage.
- Black end card with creator name reinforces studio-branding, premium finish.

## Takeaway (copy this, skip that)

- COPY: same accent shape in every scene. For us: one green circle/disc behind the hero object in every scene, same size logic, same placement logic — consistency is what makes it feel premium, not new shapes each time.
- COPY: lead-in small/grey → punch word huge/bold, about 1 second apart, inside every scene. For us: small "invests" → huge "$20M", small "lenders add" → huge "$80M".
- COPY: oversized background word drifting horizontally, cropped by frame edge, for parallax. For us: huge grey "LOOP" / "RISK" drifting behind the main diagram.
- COPY: poster changes every 2–4 seconds, with a build inside each poster. Never a 20–40 second still.
- COPY: tiny print details in corners (scale, sheet, source) — we already have this in Mix 3, keep it, make it consistent.
- SKIP: cut-out photographs, film stills, astronaut, fox character — those are that creator's subjects, not ours. Use the poster mechanic with our vector diagrams.
- SKIP: red accent — translate to Nvidia green in our dark theme, and keep it as the ONLY accent.
- SKIP: hard cuts alone — our transitions should also have a slide/scale inside the next poster, so a cut does not feel like a jump.
