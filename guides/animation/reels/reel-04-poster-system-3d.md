## Visual — what it looks like

- A consistent premium poster system, not live-action footage. Static vertical poster frame, no handheld camera.
- Restrained palette in almost every beat: pale light-grey textured paper background, deep forest green, black, white, and one hot red accent. Occasional lime-green and glowing blue/purple for a single object.
- Realistic, well-lit 3D objects with long soft drop shadows anchoring them: a pink brain, a white winged horse, a glowing butterfly, a faceted dark gem, a white chess king.
- One idea per beat, held long enough to read, then a fast wipe.

## Motion — how it moves

- Main moves: slide, scale, and 3D rotate. Fade/blur for transitions, not hard jump cuts alone.
- Tiles and text slide in vertically/horizontally, with visible motion blur in the fastest moments.
- Orbit dots rotate continuously around the brain — the only continuous loop motion in the reel.
- 3D objects (horse, gem, chess king) scale up and rotate in 3D as they land, with a slight settle/overshoot feel, not a hard stop.
- Butterfly glows, hand rises into a black panel, a small red line slides in at the foot of a poster.
- One transition is a fast wipe of vertical colour panels (grey, white, red, dark green).
- Text is built in stages: a tiny lead-in first, then a huge contrasting hero word that fills most of the frame width. Size jump is deliberate.
- Camera effect is simulated inside the poster: objects scale toward the viewer and rotate in 3D, panels slide across a fixed frame. Viewpoint itself does not move.

## Font and size

- Deliberate mix, not one house font: tall condensed sans for the big headlines, heavy bold sans for the main concepts, elegant script for one hero word, handwritten for one sub-word, small plain sans for lead-ins.
- Hierarchy by scale AND colour: lead-ins roughly one-quarter the height of the hero word, hero word large enough to bleed to the frame edges, red reserved for the word the viewer must remember.
- This is the opposite of our last video, where all text was one size and one weight.

## Timing / pacing (estimates)

- Each poster beat holds roughly 2–4 seconds.
- Transitions well under 1 second.
- Whole loop felt like roughly half a minute to under a minute, but that is an estimate, not a measured duration.

## Why it feels premium

- Limited palette and repeated grey/green/red system across every beat.
- Consistent long shadows and realistic 3D lighting.
- Eased, overshooting entrances that settle, not abrupt stops.
- Motion blur on fastest slides, so they read as intentional, not glitchy.
- One idea per beat, held long enough to read, then a fast wipe.
- Huge hero word vs tiny lead-in creates a clear, confident rhythm.
- Core argument of the reel: motion is not decoration, it is strategy that moves.

## Takeaway (copy this, skip that)

- COPY: tiny lead-in + huge hero word, per beat. For us: small "Nvidia invests" then huge "$20M", small "lenders add" then huge "$80M", small "total" then huge "$100M".
- COPY: one idea per beat, 2–4 seconds, then a fast wipe. Never hold one still scene for 20–40 seconds.
- COPY: 3D objects scale + rotate in with overshoot and settle, with long soft shadows. For us: GPU chip, rack, domino — same entrance, dark stage, green accent.
- COPY: continuous motion in at least one element per beat (orbit dots, glowing butterfly) so the frame is never fully still.
- COPY: red reserved for the remember-word → translate to Nvidia green reserved for the remember-number/word in our dark theme.
- SKIP: the brand-product grid, brain, horse, butterfly, gem, and chess king themselves — those are that creator's metaphors, not ours. Use the mechanic, not the objects.
- SKIP: script/handwritten fonts for a finance explainer — keep our clean sans, but vary size and weight as above.
