## Visuals

- Vertical documentary/editorial motion poster, not a fast social edit.
- Very limited premium palette: deep oxblood/dark red-brown and near-black backgrounds, off-white/cream typography, muted natural skin tones, black-and-white halftone illustrations, red only as an accent.
- Contrast throughout: polished glossy beauty imagery set against darker compositions — aged face, skull, microscope, serum dropper.
- Visible film/halftone grain, soft vignetting, generous dark negative space — large type feels like a magazine cover.

## Animation

- Dominant movements are slow scale/zoom, fade, and cross-dissolve, not fast pops or morphs.
- In one scene, simulated camera pushes in: products slide out to the left while a face grows larger in frame.
- In another, question-mark shapes and small surrounding words fade/pop in one by one over a long hold, rather than all appearing at once.
- Microscope and serum scenes use slow push-in and gentle drift of the halftone graphic.
- Transitions between poster compositions are soft dissolves, or a large type/graphic sliding across the frame, rather than hard jump cuts.
- No obvious live-action camera pan. Movement is simulated in post: slow push-ins, very slow zoom in/out, lateral slides of large type and halftone graphics. Portrait itself stays largely centred and still, keeping motion controlled.

## Font and size

- Almost all text all-caps, condensed editorial serif with strong thick/thin contrast.
- Hero words very large, often filling a third to a half of frame width.
- Secondary words much smaller, sometimes faded to grey, placed scattered around the portrait like magazine annotations.
- One small word picked out in red while the rest of its line is cream/white, creating hierarchy.
- Size changes move the eye from hook word to explanatory small print, not for decorative bouncing.

## Timing (estimates)

- Slow for a reel. Major compositions held roughly 6–15 seconds or more in sampling; one composition held longest, still on screen after successive 8- and 12-second samples, with only its shapes/words becoming clearer.
- Within a composition, smaller changes — a word fading in, a dotted line appearing, a slow zoom — happen over several seconds, not fractions of a second.

## Why it feels premium

- Restricted palette, consistent typography and halftone texture across every scene.
- Slow eased zooms and staggered fades instead of jittery cuts.
- Scale contrast — one huge word against tiny scattered labels.
- Each poster held long enough to read it.
- Editorial contrast, rather than speed, carries the argument.

## Takeaway (visuals + animation only)

- COPY: slow push-in on a centred portrait/diagram, with products/elements sliding out to the side as the hero grows — for us: data-centre diagram pushing in while side labels slide away, revealing the loop.
- COPY: small scattered annotation words fading in one by one over a long hold — for us: small labels around a diagram ("debt", "interest", "empty racks") fading in one by one, not all at once.
- COPY: one hero word huge, secondary words tiny and faded, red/green only for the hook word — for us: huge "LOOP" or "RISK", tiny annotations around it, green only for the hook.
- COPY: soft dissolve or large type sliding across the frame as a transition, instead of a hard jump cut, at section breaks.
- SKIP: slow 6–15 second holds alone — in our explainer, pair a slow zoom with a new fading label every 2–4 seconds, or a slow hold will feel still, as in our last video.
- SKIP: oxblood/cream/halftone palette and editorial serif — translate to dark stage, white/grey, one green accent, clean sans, same scale-contrast logic.
