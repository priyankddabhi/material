## Visuals

- Flat minimal premium kinetic-typography piece, built almost entirely from graphic layers, not filmed footage.
- Palette chiefly warm cream/off-white, black, bright signal red, and white, with photographic cut-outs of money stacks and a black human silhouette.
- Recurring graphic furniture: faint dotted grid, small barcodes, thin curved lines and orbit circles, red circular icon badges, small HD/4K-style marks, vertical edge text, black pill-shaped button with hand-cursor graphic.
- No conventional camera movement: movement comes from animated layers, panel wipes, and slow drift/scale of money and silhouette elements, not a panning/zooming camera.

## Animation

- Dominant motion is kinetic typography: words pop/scale in, often one phrase at a time, key words enlarged and recoloured for emphasis, rather than long sentences scrolling across.
- Supporting elements fade or slide in more gently; money stacks drift and scale slightly; orbit badges sit around the silhouette.
- Scene changes use clean hard cuts and black/cream panel wipes.
- Within a beat, motion is smooth and eased — elements settle rather than stopping abruptly.
- Text entrances and scene changes coincide with voiceover beats and musical accents; no dialogue was out of sync with visible text. Repeated vocal/music fragments at loop points function as rhythmic accents.
- Each spoken phrase/text beat lasts roughly 2–4 seconds, so the reel feels brisk but readable; no rapid strobing or jump-cut montage.

## Font and size

- Clean bold sans-serif throughout.
- Hierarchy by size and colour, not by changing typeface: small supporting lines modest and black/white, ordinary phrases medium-weight, emotional/selling words several times larger, extra-bold, and red.
- Capitalisation used for the final call-to-action word.

## Timing (estimates)

- Each phrase/text beat roughly 2–4 seconds.
- Full loop roughly 25–30 seconds per pass, estimated, not measured.

## Why it feels premium

- Generous spacing, consistent alignment, restrained three-colour palette.
- Smooth eased motion, elements settle rather than stopping abruptly.
- One phrase at a time, so each beat is readable.
- Text landing exactly on voiceover beats, not floating free of the narration.

## Takeaway (visuals + animation only)

- COPY: kinetic typography synced to narration — each narration phrase gets its own text beat, key word several times larger and in green, supporting words small. For us: narration line "Nvidia invests $20M" → small "Nvidia invests", huge green "$20M", landing on the spoken number.
- COPY: one phrase at a time, 2–4 seconds per beat, clean hard cut or panel wipe between beats — this is the closest model to our narration-driven explainer.
- COPY: orbit badges around a central silhouette/diagram — for us: orbit badges around a central data-centre or chip (money, debt, demand, price) sitting around the hero, not scattered randomly.
- COPY: money stacks drifting/scaling slightly during a hold, so a hold is never fully still.
- COPY: split composition (dark band top, light middle, dark band bottom) for a key idea — for us: dark top "they pay for", huge green middle "who they become" equivalent = "real customers", dark bottom supporting line.
- SKIP: cream/red palette, money-stack and silhouette subjects, and the sales call-to-action button — translate to dark stage, white/grey, one green accent, and end with our question, not a sales button.
- SKIP: relying on voice sync alone — pair each synced text beat with a visual motion (bar growing, domino falling, rack assembling) so the scene moves even if the viewer watches muted.
